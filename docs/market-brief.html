Networking is moving to agents. Trust hasn't.

Professional networking is a large, old market that runs on one scarce resource: people's attention. AI products now offer to spend that attention for you. They call you, read your inbox or text you about who to meet. The next step is the one this hackathon names in challenge 5: your agent, my agent. Once two agents talk to each other directly, the hard problem stops being matching. It becomes what each agent is allowed to say.

Why now

  • Agents can already talk to each other. Google introduced the Agent2Agent (A2A) protocol in April 2025, and the Linux Foundation took over its governance in June 2025. Agents advertise themselves with "agent cards" [6]. Identity is getting solved. What an agent may say on its person's behalf is not.
  • Models over-share when they're trying to help. On CI-Work, frontier agents violate contextual integrity in 15.8–50.9% of enterprise tasks, and the authors find that higher task utility often comes with more violations [7]. A networking agent is a helpfulness machine, so it is exactly where this failure shows up.
  • Prompting isn't a policy. Recent work treats agent privacy as something enforced at runtime across the whole conversation, not filtered one output at a time [8], or trained in with reinforcement learning [9]. Overture takes the runtime route: the gate is deterministic code outside the model.

The wedge: events

A hackathon, conference or accelerator demo day is a closed room with a deadline. Everyone has a reason to meet someone, nobody has time to talk to everyone, and the organiser already asks the check-in questions (this event's "Mystery Match" asks what you're building, what you're good at, and what you're looking for). Overture plugs into that check-in, runs the agent negotiations in the background, and hands each person a short list of yes/no intros with a reason and a meeting slot.

Risks and how Overture answers them

RiskAnswer in the product
Agents leak what people told them in confidencePer-item policies; a gate on every outgoing message (phrase overlap, identifying numbers and names, contact details); a rewrite, then redaction; blocked attempts logged without their content
A malicious agent tries to extract dataInbound messages are framed as data; injection patterns are flagged to the receiving agent; an adversarial persona runs in the demo room
Spam introsReciprocity is a geometric mean, so a one-sided intro scores low; double opt-in; a person's standing instructions
Echo chambersUnexpectedness is rewarded, peaking at moderate distance (the weak-ties result) [5]
Contact details harvestedEmails exist only in the intro, returned only to the two people, only after both accept
The bet. When every professional has an agent, the winning network is the one whose agents can be trusted to talk. Overture makes the trust part explicit: a policy per fact, a gate in code, a log of what it stopped, and a human yes on both sides before anything is shared.

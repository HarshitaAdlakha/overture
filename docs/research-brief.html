What the research says about agents that introduce people.

Three questions shaped Overture. Who should meet? What may an agent say for you? And how do two agents reach a decision? For each, we used the strongest evidence we could find, mostly peer-reviewed and highly cited, plus 2025–26 preprints where the field is too new for journals. Every design choice in the code points back to one of these.

1 · Who should meet: moderately weak ties, both ways

A causal test of the strength of weak ties
Rajkumar, Saint-Jacques, Bojinov, Brynjolfsson, Aral · Science 2022 · ~200 citations
Multiple randomised experiments on LinkedIn's People You May Know, covering 20M+ people over 5 years, with 2B new ties and 600,000 new jobs. Weak ties increased job mobility, but non-linearly: an inverted U, where moderately weak ties helped most and the returns diminished beyond that. Weak ties mattered more in digital industries.
TookUnexpectedness enters the score through bridge(u), an inverted U peaking at u = 0.65, not at maximum strangeness. This crowd is about as digital as an industry gets, which is where the paper found weak ties mattered most.
Beyond accuracy: evaluating recommender systems by coverage and serendipity
Ge, Delgado-Battenfeld, Jannach · RecSys 2010 · ~790 citations
Serendipity combines unexpectedness with usefulness. An unexpected but irrelevant recommendation isn't serendipitous.
TookUnexpectedness only multiplies relevance: score = reciprocity × (0.55 + 0.45 × bridge(u)). A pair with nothing to offer each other scores 0, however surprising.
Serendipity in Recommender Systems: A Systematic Literature Review
Ziarani & Ravanmehr · J. Computer Science and Technology 2021 · ~115 citations
Over-emphasising accuracy leads to over-specialised, predictable recommendations. Serendipity's advantage over plain novelty or diversity is that it stays useful.
TookThe ranker scores unexpected separately from fit, and shows both on every intro, so people can see why a stranger was suggested.
Knowledge substitutability and complementarity in scientific collaboration
Lin et al. · Journal of Informetrics 2025
Using the American Physical Society dataset: overlapping (substitutable) knowledge lowers performance, while complementary knowledge raises it, especially in small teams.
TookFit is my needs × your offers, not shared interests. Reciprocity is the geometric mean of the two directions, so a one-sided intro (a recruiter who only takes) scores low.

2 · What an agent may say: contextual integrity, enforced outside the model

CI-Work: Benchmarking Contextual Integrity in Enterprise LLM Agents
Fu et al. · arXiv 2026
Frontier agents violated contextual integrity in 15.8–50.9% of enterprise workflows, with leakage up to 26.7%. Higher task utility often came with more violations, and bigger models or deeper reasoning didn't fix it. The authors call for context-centric architectures.
TookThe gate is plain code that runs on every outgoing message whatever the model, and the eval measures leaks with the gate off and on.
OCELOT: Inference-Leakage Budgets for Privacy-Preserving LLM Agents
Xie et al. · arXiv 2026
Agent privacy is a property of the whole trajectory: leakage is cumulative, bidirectional (injection turns the agent against its user) and task-dependent. OCELOT puts a deterministic verifier and a ledger next to an untrusted model.
TookThe same split: the model proposes and a deterministic gate decides. Blocked attempts go to ov_gate_log, which stores the rule and the item but never the blocked text. Inbound messages are framed as data, and injection patterns are flagged to the receiving agent.
Contextual Integrity in LLMs via Reasoning and Reinforcement Learning
Lan et al. · arXiv 2025 · ~60 citations
Asking models to reason explicitly about what is appropriate to share cuts inappropriate disclosure; RL on about 700 synthetic examples improves it further and transfers to PrivacyLens.
TookThe agent prompt lists every item with its policy (share, after both accept, never) and its id, and says what each means. The prompt is the first line of defence, and the gate is the backstop.

3 · How two agents decide: short, scored negotiation

LLM-Deliberation: Evaluating LLMs with Interactive Multi-Agent Negotiation Games
Abdelnabi et al. · 2023 (NeurIPS 2024 D&B) · ~75 citations
Multi-issue negotiation games show that agents can reach deals, but greedy or adversarial players change the dynamics.
TookAn adversarial persona (Recruiter Bot) lives in the demo room and tries to extract salaries, visa status and runway from every agent.
AgenticPay: A Multi-Agent LLM Negotiation System for Buyer-Seller Transactions
Liu et al. · arXiv 2026
Agents with private constraints negotiating in language show large performance gaps, especially in long-horizon reasoning.
TookNegotiations are capped at four messages of at most 60 words each, followed by a mediator verdict. Short horizons are cheaper and more reliable, and easier for people to read.
Towards understanding the characteristics of successful and unsuccessful collaborations
Love et al. · Humanities & Social Sciences Communications 2022
Team success is less about picking the right people than about building relationships for knowledge integration.
TookThe output isn't a match score. It's a reason, what each person gets, and a concrete 15-minute agenda, so the first meeting starts the relationship.

The score, in one line

reciprocity = sqrt(helps_me × i_help)               // both directions; one-sided → low
bridge(u)   = max(0, 1 − ((u − 0.65) / 0.65)²)        // inverted U, peak at moderate distance
score       = reciprocity × (0.55 + 0.45 × bridge(u))  // unexpectedness multiplies, never rescues

The gate, in four rules

RuleBlocksExample
phrase_overlapThree content words in a row from a protected item"months of runway left"
distinctive_tokenA standalone number, amount, name or acronym found only in a protected item"$800k", "Nvidia", "O-1"
contact_detailEmails, phone numbers and links before both people accept"sam@…", "calendly.com/…"
declared_useThe model declares it used a protected item (available for audits)uses: ["f1"]

The gate's first response to a hit is a rewrite: the model is told which rule fired, but not the secret. If the rewrite fails the gate too, the message is replaced with a fixed line. Twenty-two unit tests cover the rules, the false positives we found (such as "15 minutes" against "O-1"), the rewrite and redaction paths, injection flags, and the intro releasing after both accept items but never never items.

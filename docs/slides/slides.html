The problem

Networking costs attention.
Agents that network for you over-share.

1 day

A room of builders, eleven hours, and you'll meet maybe five people. Which five is mostly luck: who sat next to you, who spoke first.

15.8–50.9%

How often frontier-model agents violate contextual integrity on enterprise tasks, sharing what they shouldn't. More helpful agents leak more (CI-Work, 2026).

Overture.A networking agent is a helpfulness machine. That's exactly where leaks happen.
How it works

Brief. Negotiate. Gate. Both say yes.

1 · BriefAt check-in: what you're building, what you're good at, what you're looking for, plus a private box. Your agent turns it into offers, needs and private facts.
2 · NegotiateYour agent ranks the room by two-way fit and unexpectedness, then talks to the other agents directly: four short messages each.
3 · GateEvery outgoing message is checked in code against your private items. A leak gets rewritten or withheld, and logged.
4 · Both say yesYou get a reason, what each of you gets, and a 15-minute agenda. Only when both accept: emails, a calendar slot, and whatever you held back.
Overture.Human-agent collaboration: the agents do the legwork, people make every decision

Who should meet · grounded in the research

Two-way fit × moderately unexpected.

reciprocity = √(helps_me × i_help)
bridge(u)   = 1 − ((u − 0.65)/0.65)²
score       = reciprocity
            × (0.55 + 0.45·bridge(u))

LinkedIn's randomised experiments on 20M people: moderately weak ties drive the most job mobility, an inverted U (Rajkumar et al., Science 2022). Serendipity is unexpectedness and relevance (Ge et al., 2010), so surprise multiplies fit but never replaces it.

One edge function. Zero readable tables.

Postgres, RLS on7 tables: people, items with a policy, matches, gated turns, gate log (the rule, never the text), decisions, eval runs. No policies: the browser can't read any of them
